Firm Financial Fitness

Status: Draft one-hour training module · open to sponsors

A one-hour training module on the fundamentals a firm’s leaders need to know, or have on staff, for the firm to be financially fit to compete for capital.

Author: Noel A. Johnson, Conrad / Old i, LLC
Sponsor(s): Open. Suited to industry collective action, public programs or philanthropy.

Why this module

Mass timber firms need capital to grow: inventory, equipment, people, plants and projects. Capital is an input like any other, and it is allocated competitively. Every lender, investor, grant-maker and agency is choosing between a mass timber firm and something else it could fund.

The Industry Structure analysis shows the setting these firms compete in: strong substitutes, powerful suppliers and an industry whose attractiveness to capital is low today. Sound firms still struggle to raise money in that setting. Often the business is not the problem. The difficulty is explaining the market opportunity and the financial plan in the language and norms that capital providers use.

Many firms across the chain were built by people with deep forestry, engineering, manufacturing and construction expertise. Few have staff trained in finance, strategy and marketing to the level capital providers are used to meeting. This module is a practical first step toward closing that gap. It is built to be used, with a self-evaluation to score and a guide to follow. It is not a paper to read.

What capital asks in return

To use another party’s capital, a firm must give something in exchange. There are only three things to give, alone or in combination. Many firms in the industry have not met this idea, and fewer are comfortable with it. It is the first fundamental of the module.

OneA share of future cash flows

Interest and repayment to a lender, distributions to an owner, or a share of revenue.

TwoA share of future asset value

Ownership of part of what the firm or the asset becomes worth, or a claim on it as security.

ThreeA say in governance

Limits on the firm’s freedom to decide: covenants, board seats, approval rights, conditions and reporting.

Risk is the third dimension. Within every channel some providers fund what is new and novel, and others fund only what is proven and predictable. Which is which, for mass timber, is left open here on purpose: each firm maps it for itself, using the guide that follows.

Wayfinding: which pool of capital do you seek to swim in?

Money is already flowing through each of the six channels. A firm’s task is to divert some of that flow toward itself, which starts with knowing which pool it belongs in. Four questions locate it.

  1. What is the money for?The firm itself, its people and technology (financial assets), or a building, a line or a machine (real assets).
  2. How large is it, against what you have done before?A step of the same size as past borrowing, or an order of magnitude beyond it.
  3. How proven is the use?More of what already works, or something new to the firm or to the market.
  4. What will you give in exchange?Cash flows, asset value, governance, and how much of each.

Each pool also calls for a different person to carry the conversation: a return forecast for one, a political conversation for another, risk mitigation or reporting for a third. A firm reaches a provider in one of four ways: its leaders know them, it works with those who do, it pays an intermediary, or it pays for a guarantee.

The one-hour program

An initial session for the leaders of firms across the chain: producers, fabricators, engineers, architects, contractors and developers. It can be run in a room or online, for one firm or for a cohort.

  1. 0 – 5 minWhy capital is competed for. The industry’s structure, and who else is asking the same providers for money.
  2. 5 – 15 minWhat capital asks in return. Cash flows, asset value and governance, and how each kind of capital combines them.
  3. 15 – 25 minSix channels and wayfinding. Each leader places the firm’s next need in a channel.
  4. 25 – 45 minSelf-evaluation. Each leader scores the firm on the ten fundamentals below.
  5. 45 – 55 minKnow it, or have it on staff. Discussion of the lowest scores: learn it, hire it or retain an adviser.
  6. 55 – 60 minOne next step. Written down, with a name and a date.

Self-evaluation: ten fundamentals

The fundamentals follow the disciplines that make up the first-year MBA curriculum at leading business schools, among them Stanford Graduate School of Business and Harvard Business School: accounting, finance, data and decisions, economics, marketing, operations, strategy, strategy beyond markets, and organizational behavior and leadership. The tenth draws them together as a capital strategy. A leader does not need to master each one personally. The firm needs each one either known at the top or held by someone on staff or on retainer.

The disciplines are those of the schools’ published first-year curricula (Stanford GSB, 2025–26 offerings; Harvard Business School, 2026–27 terms). The three levels for each, and their application to mass timber firms, are the author’s and are a draft for discussion.

After the first hour

The hour locates a firm’s gaps. Closing them takes practice on the firm’s own numbers and plans. A follow-on half-day workout does that, station by station, each one framed, discussed and then applied in writing.

See the half-day workout

Station 1

Know who you are competing with for capital

Frame
Capital is allocated competitively and priced against a safe alternative. A provider asks what it earns for taking your risk instead of someone else's. Industry structure shows where bargaining power sits and why that question is hard for mass timber today.
Discuss
Who else is asking the same provider for money, and why would it choose you?
Apply
Write the case for your firm in two sentences, in the provider's terms, not the product's.

Station 2

The three levers of value

Frame
Every investment case reaches value through one of three levers: the income it produces, the cost to create it, or the return required for its risk. Price is not cost. A cost that also adds risk, even perceived risk, grows in steps and not in a straight line, which is why the team matters as much as the product.
Discuss
Which lever does your product or project move, and what evidence would a stranger accept?
Apply
List the claims you make about mass timber. Mark each against a lever. Strike the ones that reach none.

Station 3

Know your end users

Frame
Without a market there is no price signal, and without a price signal an attribute has only potential value. Mass timber answers to three end users: occupants, the savers and investors who own the asset, and the civic and political interests that permit and promote it.
Discuss
Which projects predominate in your market, and which end user were they responding to?
Apply
Name your end user in each of the three groups, and what each one pays with: rent, capital or approval.

Station 4

Your numbers, in their language

Frame
Capital providers read the same few things in any firm: how cash moves through the year, what the money will be used for and how it comes back, how much new capital the firm can put to productive use, who decides, and whether the reporting is timely and clear.
Discuss
What would a lender or investor ask first that you could not answer today?
Apply
List the three documents you would need to produce within thirty days of a serious conversation, and who in your firm would produce them.

Station 5

Choose your channel

Frame
Each of the six channels has its own costs, timelines and expectations, some written and some not, and each asks for a different mix of cash flows, asset value and governance.
Discuss
What capital do you use now, for operations and for long-lived assets, and what do you need next?
Apply
Place your next request in one of the six channels. Mark it higher or lower risk. Name the person who will carry it, and two comparable deals a provider would recognise.

Close

The room is the panel

Share
There is no outside panel. Participants report what they found at each station, so that every firm learns from the others.
Leave with
A one-page capital plan: the channel, the request, the evidence still missing, and the next step with a date.

Ground rules

How the room works

  • Nobody needs to have all the answers.
  • A firm's details stay with the firm. Nothing is collected for reporting.
  • Help is offered only where a firm asks for it.
  • Everyone shares, on the expectation that a rising tide lifts all.

What this is not

  • An introduction service to investors or lenders. Each firm builds those relationships itself, works with those who have them, or pays professionals who do.
  • A first course in development or in running a plant. It assumes participants know their own business.

Who could sponsor it

The module builds the industry's professional capacity in finance and business, which no single firm has reason to pay for alone and all of them benefit from. That makes it a natural intervention for any party seeking to accelerate the industry's growth.

Industry collective action

Associations and producer groups, as a shared investment in the capability of their members.

Public policy

Economic and workforce development programs that want firms able to absorb and repay growth capital.

Philanthropy

Funders pursuing climate, forest or rural-economy outcomes that depend on a financially capable industry.

A sponsor would fund facilitation, materials and a cohort of firms, starting with the one-hour session. What each cohort learns, kept anonymous, also sharpens the working group's research agenda on capital access.
