Firm Financial Fitness

Status: Draft training module · open to sponsors

A basic training module that builds the financial acumen of mass timber firms, so they can compete for the capital they need to grow.

Author: Noel A. Johnson, Conrad / Old i, LLC
Sponsor(s): Open. Suited to industry collective action, public programs or philanthropy.

Why this module

Mass timber firms need capital to grow: inventory, equipment, people, plants and projects. Capital is an input like any other, and it is allocated competitively. Every lender, investor, grant-maker and agency is choosing between a mass timber firm and something else it could fund.

The Industry Structure analysis shows the setting these firms compete in: strong substitutes, powerful suppliers and an industry whose attractiveness to capital is low today. Sound firms still struggle to raise money in that setting. Often the business is not the problem. The difficulty is explaining the market opportunity and the financial plan in the language and norms that capital providers use.

Many firms across the chain were built by people with deep forestry, engineering, manufacturing and construction expertise. Few have staff trained in finance, strategy and marketing to the level capital providers are used to meeting. This module is a practical step toward closing that gap. It is a workout, with something to do at every station. It is not a paper to read.

Risk is the third dimension. Within every channel some providers fund what is new and novel, and others fund only what is proven and predictable. Which is which, for mass timber, is left open here on purpose: participants map it for their own firm at Station 5.

The workout

About half a day, in person, for the leaders of firms across the chain: producers, fabricators, engineers, architects, contractors and developers. Each of five stations follows the same three moves. A topic is framed in a short teaching segment, discussed at the table, then applied by each participant to their own firm or project, in writing.

Station 1

Know who you are competing with for capital

Frame
Capital is allocated competitively and priced against a safe alternative. A provider asks what it earns for taking your risk instead of someone else's. Industry structure shows where bargaining power sits and why that question is hard for mass timber today.
Discuss
Who else is asking the same provider for money, and why would it choose you?
Apply
Write the case for your firm in two sentences, in the provider's terms, not the product's.

Station 2

The three levers of value

Frame
Every investment case reaches value through one of three levers: the income it produces, the cost to create it, or the return required for its risk. Price is not cost. A cost that also adds risk, even perceived risk, grows in steps and not in a straight line, which is why the team matters as much as the product.
Discuss
Which lever does your product or project move, and what evidence would a stranger accept?
Apply
List the claims you make about mass timber. Mark each against a lever. Strike the ones that reach none.

Station 3

Know your end users

Frame
Without a market there is no price signal, and without a price signal an attribute has only potential value. Mass timber answers to three end users: occupants, the savers and investors who own the asset, and the civic and political interests that permit and promote it.
Discuss
Which projects predominate in your market, and which end user were they responding to?
Apply
Name your end user in each of the three groups, and what each one pays with: rent, capital or approval.

Station 4

Your numbers, in their language

Frame
Capital providers read the same few things in any firm: how cash moves through the year, what the money will be used for and how it comes back, how much new capital the firm can put to productive use, who decides, and whether the reporting is timely and clear.
Discuss
What would a lender or investor ask first that you could not answer today?
Apply
List the three documents you would need to produce within thirty days of a serious conversation, and who in your firm would produce them.

Station 5

Choose your channel

Frame
Each of the six channels has its own costs, timelines and expectations, some written and some not. Each also needs a different person to carry the conversation: a return forecast for one, a political conversation for another, risk mitigation or reporting for a third. There are four ways to reach a provider: know them yourself, work with those who do, pay an intermediary, or pay for a guarantee.
Discuss
What capital do you use now, for operations and for long-lived assets, and what do you need next?
Apply
Place your next request in one of the six channels. Mark it higher or lower risk. Name the person who will carry it, and two comparable deals a provider would recognise.

Close

The room is the panel

Share
There is no outside panel. Participants report what they found at each station, so that every firm learns from the others.
Leave with
A one-page capital plan: the channel, the request, the evidence still missing, and the next step with a date.

Ground rules

How the room works

  • Nobody needs to have all the answers.
  • A firm's details stay with the firm. Nothing is collected for reporting.
  • Help is offered only where a firm asks for it.
  • Everyone shares, on the expectation that a rising tide lifts all.

What this is not

  • An introduction service to investors or lenders. Each firm builds those relationships itself, works with those who have them, or pays professionals who do.
  • A first course in development or in running a plant. It assumes participants know their own business.

Who could sponsor it

The module builds the industry's professional capacity in finance and business, which no single firm has reason to pay for alone and all of them benefit from. That makes it a natural intervention for any party seeking to accelerate the industry's growth.

Industry collective action

Associations and producer groups, as a shared investment in the capability of their members.

Public policy

Economic and workforce development programs that want firms able to absorb and repay growth capital.

Philanthropy

Funders pursuing climate, forest or rural-economy outcomes that depend on a financially capable industry.

A sponsor would fund facilitation, materials and a cohort of firms. What each cohort learns, kept anonymous, also sharpens the working group's research agenda on capital access.
