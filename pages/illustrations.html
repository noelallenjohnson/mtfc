Some admired mass timber buildings are market-rate and market-bound: private capital expecting occupants to pay for the value. Others answer to non-market forces, usually civic or political. Knowing which is which changes what a project can teach. This collection will grow as researchers contribute new cases.

Global Perspectives

Mass Timber Around the World

How mass timber shows up across residential and commercial markets, and in the public, cultural, and civic spaces beyond them.

Market-rate, market-bound

United States

Ascent MKE

Market-rate

Value sought: Buyers’ willingness to pay.

A 25-story mixed-use tower in Milwaukee, Ascent was the world's tallest mass timber building at its 2022 completion, demonstrating engineered wood's viability for high-rise residential and hospitality development.

Norway

Mjøstårnet

Market-rate

Value sought: Sales, rents and hotel income, with regional timber pride alongside.

At 18 stories, Mjøstårnet in Brumunddal helped set the modern benchmark for mass timber high-rises, combining apartments, a hotel, and offices in glulam and CLT construction.

Netherlands

Haut

Market-rate

Value sought: Buyers’ willingness to pay for timber apartments.

Amsterdam's Haut is a 21-story timber-hybrid residential tower, one of the tallest timber apartment buildings in the Netherlands, pairing CLT with a concrete core for structural performance.

Read more →

Australia

International House & Daramu House

Market-rate

Value sought: Office tenants’ willingness to pay, for an institutional owner.

Sydney's International House and neighboring Daramu House were among Australia's first commercial mass timber office buildings, proving engineered wood's viability in a market long dominated by steel and concrete.

Non-market: civic, cultural, institutional

Sweden

Sara Kulturhus

Non-market

Value sought: Civic identity and pride of place for a municipality.

Skellefteå's Sara Kulturhus (Sara Cultural Centre) combines a hotel, theatre, library and gallery in one of the tallest timber buildings in the world, showing engineered wood's potential for civic and cultural architecture.

Japan

Yusuhara Wooden Bridge Museum

Non-market

Value sought: Cultural representation, resonance and respect for local craft.

Kengo Kuma's Yusuhara Wooden Bridge Museum spans a rural road using cantilevered timber construction, celebrating local forestry and traditional Japanese carpentry in a public cultural space.

South Korea

Seoul National University, Haedong Building

Non-market

Value sought: Institutional mission and demonstration, not rent.

The Haedong Advanced Engineering Building at Seoul National University is one of Korea's first large-scale mass timber academic buildings, demonstrating engineered wood's use in institutional construction.

Canada

Richmond Olympic Oval

Non-market

Value sought: A public showcase for a region’s forestry, built for the Olympics.

Built for the 2010 Winter Olympics, the Richmond Olympic Oval's undulating wood ceiling uses beetle-killed pine, showcasing mass timber's structural and sustainable design potential for large public sporting venues.
