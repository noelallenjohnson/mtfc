Why the working group exists

Hypothesis

Mass timber needs more finance and consumer acumen

Demand-side factors are under-investigated. After decades of impressive and successful R&D and funding, supply-side considerations — from design and engineering to manufacturing, construction and regulation — may no longer be the industry's main headwind. Western capital markets are deep, sophisticated and open to mass timber-related investment; they are neither for nor against it. What is missing is applied financial and consumer insight. Within the mass timber ecosystem, finance and marketing expertise has had far less investment than engineering and forestry, and it shows: these questions are often addressed late, or by analogy.

Because financial and consumer concepts feel familiar from everyday life, they're easy to misapply. The result is delayed capital formation: developers lack clear consumer value-creation frameworks, investors struggle to underwrite risk and return, research is often asked to answer finance and consumer questions it was not designed for, and policy has had little market evidence to draw on, so it rests more on goals than on measured outcomes. For any mass timber value proposition, the outstanding question is how it maps to a financial model: which assumption is adjusted, and by how much?

Response

A credible demand-side platform can help

Through its finance and consumer focus, MTFC is an attempt to examine mass timber as an economic product operating within consumer, investment and competitive markets. This is alongside, but distinct from the more familiar treatment of mass timber as a construction technology, environmental solution or public policy response. To do so, MTFC aims to provide an impartial platform at the intersection of academic research, institutional investment and real estate development, and to convene safe-harbor working groups to define and support useful applied research.

Complementary functions:

  • Establish more holistic, business-centric discussions and theses about the industry's development.
  • Serve as a repository of useful, rigorous, applied research and practitioner insights (for example, Business Case Studies and Listening to Investors).
  • Welcome in collaboration among academics, developers and capital markets professionals.
  • Provide educational frameworks and interactive financial tools to inform industry and firm growth (for example, Firm Financial Fitness, the Mass Timber Economic Taxonomy and Industry Structure).
  • Identify structural industry headwinds, complications and responses (for example, the Market Rate Development Risk Mitigation Method).

The hope is that MTFC becomes a place where researchers and practitioners come not necessarily to find favorable information about mass timber, but to find better questions about its economics, more credible evidence and more useful ways of thinking about its future. In time, it could evolve from a working group and public knowledge platform into an international executive intelligence network, helping CEOs, CFOs, COOs, commercial leaders and general managers navigate organizational transformation, digital and AI evolution, competitive markets and growth capital considerations. The purpose would be to improve business decisions and organizational performance, rather than to promote mass timber adoption. Its research would remain independent, with findings and recommendations attributable to individual authors.
