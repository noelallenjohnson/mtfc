Why the working group exists

Hypothesis

Mass timber needs more finance and consumer acumen

Demand-side factors are under-investigated. After decades of impressive and successful R&D and funding, supply-side considerations — from design and engineering to manufacturing, construction and regulation — may no longer be the industry's main headwind. Western capital markets are deep, sophisticated and open to mass timber-related investment; they are neither for nor against it. What is missing is applied financial and consumer insight. Within the mass timber ecosystem, finance and marketing expertise has had far less investment than engineering and forestry, and it shows: these questions are often addressed late, or by analogy.

Because financial and consumer concepts feel familiar from everyday life, they're easy to misapply. The result is delayed capital formation: developers lack clear consumer value-creation frameworks, investors struggle to underwrite risk and return, research is often asked to answer finance and consumer questions it was not designed for, and policy has had little market evidence to draw on, so it rests more on goals than on measured outcomes. For any mass timber value proposition, the outstanding question is how it maps to a financial model: which assumption is adjusted, and by how much?

Response

A credible demand-side platform can help

MTFC provides the impartial platform and working group needed to align academic-quality insight with institutional investment requirements and real estate development realities. It operates at the intersection of academic research, institutional investment and real estate development as they bear on the mass timber ecosystem, and it convenes safe-harbor working groups to define, support and disseminate the applied research that is needed.
