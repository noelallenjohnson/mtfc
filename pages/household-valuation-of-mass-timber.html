Underwriting Mass Timber Apartments

Status: Write-up released for public review

Value Creation and Mass Timber Apartment Renters — a research write-up of an ongoing consumer willingness-to-pay study’s initial findings across revealed and stated preferences of market-rate mass timber apartment renters, translated to the proforma.

Author(s): Noel A. Johnson, Justin Angle, Talia Esarey
Sponsor(s): Conrad Investment Management (Old i, LLC), with in-kind support from the ownership and management of Wilson Forest Park Townhomes and The Canyons, Portland, Oregon

Abstract

Across mass timber’s growing ecosystem, demand-side insights focused on end-user drivers remain largely unstudied, misunderstood and anecdotal. Lacking a data-driven business case, firms, investors and governments face material headwinds. To accelerate their business planning, underwriting and policy design interests, this research advances consumer willingness-to-pay topics in the context of two market-rate mass timber apartment developments. Common mass timber industry value-creation claims are tested, and mechanisms identified, to better answer the technical questions of capital providers.

From the 1,500 codable data points collected from 26 renters, four revealed-preference points are detected: renters’ willingness not to pay more, their tendency to stay longer, pay on time and be of high credit. The candidate value-creation mechanisms detected are experiential in nature, and include beauty, architectural differentiation and feelings of calm. These value-creation channels require wood to be visible, thus building codes that require mass timber to be covered likely hinder its market-rate adoption. Little consumer-created value is observable from credence attributes like heritage and cultural connection, carbon footprints, locally made or personal identity value-signaling. No downside consumer impacts are detected, suggesting the story is one of positive option value.

All impacts detected are mapped to development proformas (retention, turnover cost, concessions, credit loss and marketing) to show how to underwrite mass timber’s business case. All findings are directional in nature; their sizing is left to a larger study extension.
